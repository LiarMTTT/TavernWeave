# Character design

Use this reference to turn an author's ideas into playable character material. It
applies to text, MVU Zod and hybrid cards; it does not require variables, extra
worldbook entries or a new workshop workflow. Keep decisions and sources in the
existing [creative authority](creative-authority.md) and material index. Use the
optional [design worksheet](../assets/templates/character-design.md) only to the
depth the task needs.

## 1. Creative scope and method

Read the existing brief before asking questions. Distinguish the requested work:

| Request | Agent responsibility |
|---|---|
| Organize supplied material | Clarify, classify and deduplicate without inventing facts, motives or causal links. Preserve unresolved ambiguity. |
| Develop ideas together | Offer bounded proposals where useful; keep alternatives distinct from confirmed facts and reuse the author's decisions. |
| Create within an agreed brief | Write a coherent draft within that brief. Do not demand that the author supply every sentence first; surface material departures or unresolved creative choices. |

A narrow edit does not authorize redesign. Ask only questions whose answers change
the result; do not restart a confirmed interview, impose a questionnaire or keep
asking after the author deliberately leaves something open. Generated proposals,
interview answers and example scenes are not evidence of previously established
facts. Follow the existing authority's confirmation rules.

The recommended method uses four content responsibilities below, adapted from
Moyue's newer character-habitat method. They are not four mandatory files, entries,
headings or equally long forms. A compact character may need only stable facts and
disposition. Older palette, three-facet and emotion-threshold methods may help an
author who chooses them, but do not automatically add color hierarchies, required
facets, fixed reaction tables or a second interpretation layer. Existing effective
cards keep their structure unless conversion is requested.

## 2. Four content responsibilities

### Stable identity and facts

Keep enough identity, body, ability, history and social context to recognize the
character. Include details because they affect recognition, choices or interaction,
not to fill a complete biography. Separate objective history from the character's
interpretation of it; neither occupation nor appearance proves a personality.

Distinguish stable facts from facts likely to expire during the intended story:
current location, an ongoing task, a relationship stage or a temporary condition.
Place changing facts in the appropriate opening, narrative record or existing state
layer. Do not add MVU just to make this distinction. Stable shared history may remain
in the profile while present attitudes and stages change. A reference image's pose,
lighting or one outfit does not automatically become a permanent trait.

### Ongoing life and practical conditions

If an identity label does not give the character enough to act on, add the relevant
ongoing work, responsibilities, relationships, skills, places, resources and limits.
Explain what the character can actually do, what depends on them and where their
ability stops. A profession alone is not a complete skill set.

The character should have reasons to act outside the player's immediate attention
when the creative premise supports this. Important relationships may still dominate
their life; do not weaken them merely to prove independence or invent an unrelated
subplot. This material may be merged with identity. It is not a compulsory schedule,
inventory, world encyclopedia or full life history.

### Disposition, interpretation and priorities

Keep useful personality words, then establish what they mean for this particular
person: what they notice, how they interpret events, what matters to them and how
they understand themselves. Several priorities can pull against each other without
requiring trauma, a secret identity or an opposite personality.

Describe tendencies and genuine boundaries rather than preselecting every answer.
The same disposition can produce different actions as relationships, stakes, bodily
condition and experience change. Use absolute wording only for an actual absolute
boundary. Leave undecided history and future growth open; do not fill them to explain
every trait. A character can misunderstand themselves and need not narrate a complete
self-diagnosis whenever a related event occurs.

### Expression in a scene

Add this material only when the other content leaves an important expression gap.
Consider what speech accomplishes, how familiarity and stakes affect its extent,
how feeling develops over time, and how the character uses their body and surroundings.
Describe a range and its conditions, not a menu of lines, gestures or emotion stages.

Quietness is not necessarily passivity; action is not necessarily a dramatic event.
A reserved character may explain an important matter at length, while an outgoing
character may fall silent. Do not replace all restraint with loudness or require
every response to launch a new plot. Retain deliberate voice anchors and meaningful
habits, but avoid making one example the answer to every scene.

## 3. Supporting NPCs

Scale effort to the NPC's actual role. A few connected sentences may establish:

- who they are and their place in the setting;
- something they handle, want or remain responsible for outside the main interaction;
- a useful disposition or way of making choices;
- how they enter this story, what they can affect and what limits them.

These are functions, not required headings. Stop when the NPC can be recognized and
used at the requested depth. Do not turn every supporting actor into a protagonist
dossier, force a dramatic flaw, or assume special affection or hostility toward the
player. An archetype can be a starting point; do not reject a desired character merely
because the author uses words such as rational, gifted or aloof. Ask for or propose
specific meaning only where the label is insufficient for the task.

## 4. Appearance and clothing when useful

Keep stable recognition anchors separate from a particular scene's presentation.
Where clothing matters, prefer the factors that shape choices: activity, weather,
comfort, role, means, culture and intended self-presentation. Include only relevant,
established factors, or clearly proposed ones within the creative brief.

Distinguish long-term style, meaningful fixed garments or equipment, current clothing
and an actual gameplay inventory. Uniforms and signature items may be stable facts;
dress-up or collection gameplay may require specific outfits and stock. Do not ban
such content or replace it with abstract style advice. Conversely, do not invent a
wardrobe list or permanent outfit for a card that has no need for one.

## 5. Fit the card and finish

Keep common world facts, the player's profile, changing story state and output
instructions with their appropriate owners. Use
[player-profile-and-agency.md](player-profile-and-agency.md) for the player and
[character-stages.md](character-stages.md) only when the project needs explicit stages.
Do not repeat the same history or motive under all four responsibilities.

Write the final profile as clear character material. Keep interview transcripts,
draft alternatives, diagnostic language, test plans and author explanations outside
the model-visible profile. A structured form is useful when it improves retrieval;
natural prose and intentional literary voice remain valid. No fixed tag vocabulary
or forced YAML format is required.

Apply the [Chinese prose review](../../rewrite-natural-prose/references/natural-chinese-review.md)
to the current draft without enabling continuous rewriting. Remove unnecessary
three-part parallel labels or clauses; conjunctions and sentence breaks do not exempt
the same semantic pattern. Avoid repeated self-defence and do not append another
qualification when the sentence already conveys the boundary. Preserve actual
uncertainty and exceptions, useful personality words, and the character's voice.
Do not replace a trait or emotion with an invented gesture or incident during
polishing. Co-creation and delegated drafting still receive active proposals within
the authorized brief; those proposals are not evidence of prior established history.

Before finishing, ask whether removing a passage would lose meaningful recognition,
choice support or interaction boundaries. Merge redundant passages; reconsider an
example that only prewrites one scene. Stop when more writing would merely repeat,
invent unrequested complexity or speculate about an unobserved problem. Use
[character-review-and-correction.md](character-review-and-correction.md) for actual
feedback and optional interpretation guidance.

Apply [lorebook-and-prompts.md](lorebook-and-prompts.md) to placement and activation.
Splitting one character into several content responsibilities does not turn a
single-core-character card into a multi-character card. Do not change an inherited
card's effective entries, player setup or saves as a side effect of this method.

## 6. Authoring checks

- A simple character can finish without four headings, clothing or a stage system.
- A co-creation or delegated request receives useful proposals or a draft, not an
  endless demand for author-supplied facts.
- A trait supports unfamiliar situations without prescribing one line or gesture.
- Supporting NPCs remain proportionate to their role; intentional archetypes survive.
- A reference image does not silently freeze current clothing or expression.
- Old effective palette-based material survives a narrow unrelated edit.
- Static review is reported as static review; prose quality and target-model
  behaviour are not proven by the presence of these sections.
